How to read this drawing

Four bands, top to bottom. Governance sets the risk appetite. Domain personas are how a team joins the system — NOC and WG2 defined it, SOC and WG3 applied the same template, and every dashed card is proof the template doesn't change per domain. The plane is the one piece every persona shares. The bottom band is what no agent ever reaches directly.

Two arrow colors carry the whole argument. Green dashed arrows are enrollment — a persona joining the plane, mediated at every action. Grey arrows are governed access — the plane reaching into identity, data, tools, or models on an agent's behalf, never the agent reaching in on its own.

The orange border on the bottom row is deliberate. It marks the boundary this whole requirements set is built to hold: identity systems, rules, tools, and models that agents can observe or use, but never touch directly. Every WG1, WG2, and WG3 requirement in the other five reports in this set exists to keep that boundary true.

Where this feeds the rest of the set

The four plane components here are WG1's TF-1–5, REG-1–5, RS-1–6, and POR-1 in the WG1 requirements document. The persona/loop/enforcement pattern on the domain-persona cards is what WG2 and WG3 each turn into their own PER, ACC, RES, and LOOP requirements.
