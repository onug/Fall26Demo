The 25 — Crosswalk & Provenance

Source: WG1 — Agentic Overlay Alignment Analysis· dated 2026-07-21· crosswalk 2026-09-15· updated to 25 items 2026-09-23· owner: ONUG Collaborative· credit: Mick Currey & Carlos Matos, ONUG Board Members; Baird Kaake, Independent AI & Cybersecurity Researcher
Twenty-four items were scoped for WG1. The AOMC Threat & Risk Scenario Catalog added three more to cover gaps the original list didn't reach, then two same-family pairs were consolidated back down — landing at twenty-five. Some became WG1's own control-plane requirements; some got inherited by WG2's NOC persona and WG3's SOC persona; some are enterprise-level artifacts no single domain needs to rebuild. This is the one table that shows which is which — and who the list came from in the first place.

Provenance

The original 25 items come from the working group's own scoping exercise, consolidated by this project — not WG1-ratified.

  • The original sixteen high-level categories (items #1–16) are credited in the source document to Mick Currey and Carlos Matos, ONUG Board Members, dated 7/14/2026. Mick and Carlos are co-leads for WG1.
  • Also credited: Baird Kaake, Independent AI & Cybersecurity Researcher.
  • Items #17–24 were added beyond the original sixteen within the same scoping exercise.
  • 2026-09-23: the AOMC Threat Model (59 scenarios across 11 families) added Signed Step Provenance, Intermediary Identity Propagation, and Inference Result Provenance to the list, covering scenario families (chain-of-trust forgery, result integrity, design-execution divergence) that had no clean home elsewhere.
  • Same date, this project's own consolidation merges two redundant pairs — #14 Compliance Mapping + #15 Metrics/KPIs (both single-scenario governance-reporting artifacts), and #22 Aggregate Grant Risk + #23 Process Notation & Authorization Scope (already overlapping in the threat catalog's own coverage index) — landing at 25 without dropping coverage of any of the catalog's 59 scenarios. Neither the threat catalog nor this consolidation has been reviewed by Mick, Carlos, or the working group.

Scope and method

Each row below is one item from WG1's own scoping document (the Agentic Overlay Alignment Analysis, dated 2026-07-21), summarized in plain language and renumbered per this project's 24-to-25 consolidation described above. The WG1, WG2, and WG3 columns list the requirement IDs — from each working group's own requirements document — that trace back to that item: bold where the item is that requirement's primary source, muted where it's a related, secondary connection, and a dash where no current requirement in that document cites it. For merged rows (#14, #21), citations are the union of both source items' citations — nothing was invented for the merge.

The WG1 column reflects the WG1 requirements document's own citations for items #1–24. WG2 and WG3 columns reflect the lineage added to the WG2 and WG3 requirements documents after the fact — both state plainly that this mapping is analysis, not a working-group-ratified crosswalk, and that stands for this table too. Items #23–25 (added by the threat catalog) are not yet cited by any WG1, WG2, or WG3 requirements document — a different, newer gap than the pattern below.

What the empty cells mean

Seven items have no WG2 or WG3 requirement at all: #2, #3, #11, #12, #14, #21, #22.

  • Every one of the seven is a governance, process, or assurance artifact — a governance model, a risk-scoring standard, an SDLC, a test program, a compliance/metrics report, a process notation, a rights-BOM. None of them describe a runtime behavior a NOC or SOC persona performs.
  • That's a pattern, not a gap: WG1's own requirements document groups these same items under Cross-Cutting Assurance and Governance — written once, centrally, and inherited by every domain rather than re-specified per persona. WG2 and WG3 use the artifacts; they don't need to restate them as domain-specific requirements.
  • This observation is this crosswalk's own reading of the pattern, not a claim any working group has confirmed.
  • Separately, #23–25 (Signed Step Provenance, Intermediary Identity Propagation, Inference Result Provenance) have no WG1 citation either — they postdate WG1's requirements document entirely. See the row group below.

What this shows

  • Every item WG1's own scoping exercise credits still has a home in WG1 (as the merged #14 and #21 rows show by carrying both source items' citations forward). WG1's requirements document was built by numbering the same drawing's boxes against WG1's own list, so that coverage predates this update — it isn't a new claim.
  • Items #23–25 have no home anywhere yet — not WG1, not WG2, not WG3. They're the newest additions (from the AOMC Threat Model, added 2026-09-23) and postdate every current requirements document. That's a different, more open gap than the pattern below.
  • Sixteen of WG1's own scoping items also show up in WG2 and WG3 (as a primary or related citation), always identically between the two — a direct consequence of WG2 and WG3 sharing one v0.6 architecture skeleton "by explicit agreement." Where WG2 and WG3 differ is language and emphasis, not which items apply.
  • Two items are SOC-only in practice even though nothing in the source restricts them to SOC: #18 (Inferred Prompt Injection) and #19 (Credential-in-Context) appear as related citations in WG3 but only #19 appears (as related) in WG2. This crosswalk surfaces that asymmetry; it does not resolve whether WG2 should adopt #18 too.
  • Seven items never leave WG1 — see the callout above. If a future domain persona needs one of them restated as a domain-specific requirement, that would be new scope, not an oversight in this crosswalk.

Status & caveat

This table is derived, not sourced — assembled from the citations already written into the three requirements documents, not a new independent analysis. The renumbering from 24 to 25 is this project's own consolidation, not sourced from any requirements document.

  • If a citation changes in the WG1, WG2, or WG3 requirements document, this table goes stale at that cell until it's re-synced by hand.
  • As stated in all three source documents: the WG2 and WG3 item citations are this project's analysis, not reviewed or ratified by the working group. Same caveat applies to this consolidation itself.
  • Full scenario-level detail behind items #23–25 (and every other item) is in the AOMC Threat Model report.
