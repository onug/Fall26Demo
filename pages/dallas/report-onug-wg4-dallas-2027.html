1. WG4 Charter

New Working Group

WG4 defines what an AI-ready enterprise WAN must do. Enterprise WANs were sized and priced for people using cloud apps and predictable branch traffic. AI adds three traffic patterns they weren’t built for, and the result is cost, latency, and unpredictable performance:

Traffic PatternWhat Crosses the WAN
Data to computeTraining and fine-tuning data moving to wherever GPU capacity is: a cloud region, a GPU cloud, or a colocation cluster. Egress fees and transfer time are now a budget line.
Inference & agent trafficEvery site, application, and agent calling model endpoints. Latency-sensitive and hard to inspect.
Edge model rolloutWhere models run at the edge, rolling them out to sites dependably and returning curated data for retraining.

WG4 brings enterprise network architects together with carriers, colocation providers, and networking vendors. Its 2027 work has three deliverables, in order: a traffic study of what AI workloads put on member networks today, enterprise requirements for an AI-ready WAN, and an AI transport reference architecture. WG4 uses WG1’s identity, gateway, and journal rather than redefining them, and sits beside WG2, which operates the same networks.

2. What the Board Prioritized in NYC (October 27)

7 Votes · 1 Use Case

WG4 was created from one Board use case. The UC #1 submitter will be invited to co-author or serve as a Board Advisor to WG4.

Use CaseVotesWhat It Asks
UC #1 Distributed AI & Semantic Networking7Enterprises run inference at the edge (factories, branches, clinics) and train centrally. Moving raw data up and model weights down over traditional WAN links causes high bandwidth cost, tail latency, and models drifting out of sync.

The Charter Review Reframed UC #1

The WG4 and WG5 Charter Review (November 10) found that edge inference generally reduces WAN traffic, and model weights are comparatively small and move infrequently. The larger enterprise load is data moving to GPU capacity, plus inference and agent traffic. The traffic study will test both views.

4. Potential Paths Forward

Decision for Founding Leads

The charter puts the traffic study first. The choice is how much more WG4 brings to Dallas, five months after forming, and what moves to the second half of 2027.

ONUG leadership is considering another working demo for Dallas. WG4 has no lab or reference implementation, so each card says what it could show instead.

Path 1 · Charter default

Traffic Study

Dallas: Present the member traffic study. Requirements and reference architecture follow in the second half.

For: Follows the charter. Real data from member enterprises is something no vendor can offer, and it settles the question the Charter Review raised about UC #1.

Against: Depends on enterprises sharing traffic, egress-cost, and transfer-time data within months. A study is not a spec buyers can procure against.

Demo: No live demo. Findings presented on stage.

Path 2 · Study + buyer spec

Study + Requirements

Dallas: The traffic study plus a first draft of enterprise requirements for an AI-ready WAN.

For: Gives buyers procurement criteria at the summit and gives ONUG Connect something carriers can respond to.

Against: Requirements drafted alongside the study, rather than after it, may rest on assumptions the data later overturns.

Demo: No live demo. Findings and draft requirements presented on stage.

Path 3 · UC #1 as submitted

Edge Showcase

Dallas: A reference architecture slice for edge model rollout and data reduction, shown with carrier and vendor members.

For: Closest to what the Board submitter asked for, and the most visual of the three paths.

Against: The Charter Review found this may be the smaller WAN problem. It also skips the study the charter puts first.

Demo: Carrier and vendor members show a model rolled out to an edge site and curated data returned over the WAN. Not built; it depends on members supplying the environment.

Questions for Prospective WG4 Leads

1. PathWhich path does WG4 take to Dallas, and does it show anything live or present findings only?
2. Traffic StudyWhich traffic patterns does the study measure first, and what traffic, egress-cost, and transfer-time data can member enterprises share?
3. Board AdvisorThe UC #1 submitter will be invited to co-author or serve as a Board Advisor to WG4. The same Board member submitted UC #2, WG2’s top use case, so they could advise both groups. Who on the leads team makes the invitation, and how often do Board Advisors meet with the group?
4. Founding MembersWhich enterprise network architects (buyers), carriers and colocation providers, and networking vendors do we invite, keeping the three balanced so the output is useful for procurement?
5. LiaisonsWho from WG4 works with WG2 on shared path-health telemetry, and with ONUG Connect so WG4’s requirements inform carrier profiles?
