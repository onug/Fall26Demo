ONUG Collaborative · Road to Dallas 2027

Shape the requirements enterprises buy AI infrastructure against

The ONUG Collaborative is where large-enterprise IT leaders define what they need from AI infrastructure, and where suppliers help shape those requirements before they reach an RFP. Five working groups are building toward the AI Networking Summit Dallas, May 12–13, 2027.

What the Collaborative Is

Enterprise-Driven · Supplier-Enabled
Step 1

Enterprises define requirements

IT executive Co-Chairs lead working groups in which enterprise IT professionals define requirements for critical AI use cases.

Step 2

Suppliers vet and build with them

Suppliers vet those requirements with the practitioners, then work with the Co-Chairs on Buyers Guides that go straight into RFIs and RFPs.

Step 3

The Executive Board sets direction

The Collaborative Executive Board sets overall direction. In October it voted on 20 use cases, which chartered the five groups below.

Who Should Be Involved

Architecture · Engineering · Technical Product
Shape the Design

Architecture Leads

  • Review the reference architectures (WG1 v0.2, WG2/WG3 v0.6) and the new WG4 AI transport architecture as it is drafted.
  • Make sure the specs describe how real networks, platforms and products behave.
Prove It Live

Engineering Leads

  • Build Dallas demos with members: agent revocation, cross-domain attribution, shadow-agent discovery.
  • Measure real costs, such as post-quantum encryption on high-volume AI links.
Read the Demand

Technical Product Management

  • Hear enterprise requirements before they reach an RFP, and map them to your roadmap.
  • Help write the procurement criteria in Buyers Guides, such as WG5’s dated PQC roadmaps and crypto bills of materials.

WG4 and WG5 are new and recruiting founding members now. WG1 drew 42 of 118 Board votes, the most of any group.

Why Now

Governed Autonomy
  • Demand is ahead of governance. Gartner: 71% of organizations expect to deploy agentic AI within 12 months; 21% have agentic AI governance in place.
  • The network is the constraint. The Dallas AI Networking track: “the network, not the GPU, is now the binding constraint on what an enterprise can build.”
  • PQC is a buying criterion. “A present-day procurement requirement,” with EO 14412 targeting 2030–2031.
  • Early insight. Suppliers who join “gain early insights into critical challenges and key requirements for enterprise AI adoption.”

Join the Collaborative

“Suppliers are free to submit an application to join the ONUG Collaborative.” Apply at onug.net/collaborative, or talk to the ONUG team about which working group fits. Groups that form in November have five months to show something in Dallas.
