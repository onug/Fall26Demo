WG1 for 2027: Scaling the Agentic Control Plane to Dallas

For review with WG1 Leads· 9 Board Use Cases Absorbed (42 Votes)· Peter Campbell, Head of Collaborative Working Groups

This briefing prepares WG1 (Agentic Control Plane) leaders for 2027 planning and the road to the AI Networking Summit Dallas (May 12–13, 2027, Hyatt Regency Frisco). Nine of the twenty October 27 board use cases—representing 42 of the 118 board votes cast, the largest share of any working group—describe enforcement primitives, identity bindings, or promotion evidence that belong directly inside WG1’s ratified v0.2 Unified Architecture (ratified July 28, 2026) and the onug-plane reference implementation. Rather than fragmenting the plane into separate identity or assurance groups, all nine are absorbed into WG1, and this document collects WG1 leadership input on how to sequence them alongside the seven specification tasks in Section 6 of the Governed Autonomy v3 board paper.

Why Nine Board Use Cases Stay Inside WG1

“Seven pieces of work would do most of that, and all of them belong in WG1's working document rather than in a new group… Done first, it lets the 2027 working groups spend their time onboarding the domains the architecture already draws, rather than rebuilding enforcement for each of them. The test of the plane is the one this paper sets for an enterprise: the next domain should onboard without new enforcement code.” — Governed Autonomy v3, Section 6

1. WG1’s Updated 2027 Scope: What Continues & What Folds In

Continuing Working Group

WG1 remains the foundational working group of the ONUG Collaborative: the shared identity, policy, routing, and audit layer that every domain persona runs on. For 2027, the October 27 board triage reinforces WG1’s four ratified components (Agent Trust Fabric, Registry/Personas & BOM, Runtime Supervision, and Private Open Router) with three explicit scope consolidations:

Continuing Working Group · WG1 · Core Substrate

The Agentic Control Plane (2027 Charter)

1. Agent Identity & Revocable Authority Stay in WG1: Led by UC #15 (Denis Mandich, 18 votes—the #1 voted use case on the board) and UC #13 (Vesko Pehlivanov, 2 votes), task-scoped, revocable agent identity and fleet-wide stop remain WG1’s core Agent Trust Fabric (Component #1), rather than spinning out a separate identity working group.

2. Assurance & Autonomy Ladder Promotion Fold Into WG1: UC #19 (Parantap Lahiri, 5-SLI “Nutrition Labels,” 4 votes), UC #14 (Vesko Pehlivanov, control-bypass test harness, 1 vote), and UC #4 (James Walker, signed compliance evidence, 8 votes) stay inside WG1 rather than forming a separate assurance group. Because promotion SLIs and regulator proofs are computed directly from WG1’s gateway-written journal (immudb + MinIO WORM), separating assurance from the journal would split the control loop.

3. Sovereign Network Enforcement Profile Added (UC #20 Split): From UC #20 (Travis Ewert, Digital Realty, 2 votes), WG1 adds a sovereign network enforcement profile where jurisdiction is evaluated as a per-call attribute at the gateway and network/encryption state is recorded in the compliance journal (while quantum-safe transport goes to WG5).

Full 2027 WG1 Deliverable Scope: Task-scoped, revocable agent identity and fleet-wide stop; per-step Rights-BOM and composite authority checks; the gateway-written signed compliance journal; Autonomy Ladder promotion evidence (five-SLI Nutrition Label and control-bypass harness); the Private Open Router (routing, residency, budgets); sector regulatory mappings (Financial Services + Aerospace & Defense + DORA); and the sovereign network enforcement profile.

Board backing: 42 votes across 9 use cases · Delivery schedule: to be proposed by WG1 leaders

3. Aligning the 9 Use Cases with Section 6’s Seven Specification Tasks

2027 Work Plan Inputs

Section 6 of the Governed Autonomy v3 board paper lists seven concrete pieces of specification work for WG1. Every one of the nine absorbed board use cases reinforces or completes one of those seven tasks:

Section 6 Specification Task What It Delivers Aligned Board Use Cases
1. Rights & Normal-Behavior Spec Close WG1’s open Registry/BOM gap: machine-readable per-step Rights-BOM, BPMN 2.0 scope notation, composite-authority bounds, and normal-behavior baseline schema. UC #6 (Baird Kaake), UC #13 (Vesko Pehlivanov), UC #15 (Denis Mandich)
2. Unified Vocabulary & Decision-Rights Matrix Standardize Agent Class (what it is, 1–5), Risk Tier (what it could break, 1–4), and Autonomy Rung (L0–L3), plus the promotion evidence required to advance a rung. UC #9 (Satish Natarajan), UC #14 (Vesko Pehlivanov), UC #19 (Parantap Lahiri)
3. Policy-as-Code Standard Changes Count a pre-approved, policy-as-code standard change enforced at the gateway as human approval (ITIL standard-change model), and track approval latency as a KPI. UC #15 (Denis Mandich), UC #19 (Parantap Lahiri)
4. Gateway-Held Secrets & Gateway-Written Journal Codify that secret rotation (Item #5) applies to gateway-held credentials and compliance evidence (Item #9) must be written by the gateway outside agent reach, including portable Merkle proofs. UC #4 (James Walker), UC #13 (Vesko Pehlivanov), UC #20 (Travis Ewert)
5. Bringing Vendor-Embedded Agents Under the Plane Common procurement and onboarding requirements so third-party SaaS and infrastructure vendors’ embedded agents authenticate, route, and journal through the enterprise plane. UC #9 (Satish Natarajan), UC #15 (Denis Mandich), UC #20 (Travis Ewert)
6. Fleet-Wide Stop, Federation & Sovereign Profile Specify the fleet-wide stop-all SLA, federated multi-plane operation (“no über-controller”), the plane’s own security baseline, and per-call jurisdiction enforcement. UC #15 (Denis Mandich), UC #20 (Travis Ewert)
7. Private Open Router & Regulatory Crosswalk Specify governed model routing (cost, risk, sovereignty, certification) and extend Item #14’s regulatory mappings to DORA, US interagency third-party guidance, and Aerospace & Defense. UC #3 (James Walker), UC #9 (Satish Natarajan), UC #20 (Travis Ewert)

4. How WG1 Aligns With & Enables WG2, WG3, WG4, and WG5

Cross-Group Architecture

WG1 is the shared substrate underneath the other working groups. Keeping the boundaries crisp prevents scope creep into WG1 while ensuring every domain group builds on WG1’s primitives:

Sibling Group What WG1 Provides to That Group What That Group Owns (Outside WG1)
WG2 Continue
Autonomous Operations (NOC + SOC Personas)		 Provides the Access Enforcement & Audit wrapper (Layer 3) underneath WG2’s personas: SPIRE SVIDs, OpenBao credential injection, MCP gateway mediation, immudb journaling, and the 5-SLI promotion scorecard. Owns the NOC persona (Step 4 Verify Gate, digital-twin pre-change validation, cross-domain telemetry correlation in UC #2, #10, #12, #17, #18) and the SOC persona moved from WG3 (Step 4 Decide Gate).
WG3 Updated
AI Security: AI Agents as Insider Threats		 Enforces pre-execution and runtime policy at the gateway and writes the tamper-evident compliance journal that WG3 investigators consume as forensic chain-of-custody evidence. Owns in-policy agent misuse (harmful actions that pass gateway policy), unsanctioned/shadow agents acting under employee credentials outside the gateway (UC #5), and extending HR/Legal/Security insider-risk programs to non-human insiders.
WG4 New
AI WAN		 Clean separation: WG4 is a wide-area transport data-plane problem, so it does not touch or duplicate WG1’s identity, gateway policy, or compliance journal. Owns enterprise AI WAN requirements and the AI transport reference architecture (semantic data reduction in flight and deterministic sync between edge inference and central training, UC #1).
WG5 New
Post-Quantum Security for AI Infrastructure		 Two-way plug-in: (1) WG1 hosts WG5’s Crypto-Discovery & Migration Persona (UC #8) as an onboarded domain persona on the plane; (2) WG1’s Agent Trust Fabric adopts WG5’s post-quantum profile. Defines post-quantum SPIFFE SVIDs, ML-DSA signed A2A Cards and AIBOMs (UC #7), the agentic crypto-migration playbook (UC #8), and resilient key management across distributed AI infrastructure (UC #16) paced to EO 14412 and CISA CBOM deadlines.

5. Input Needed from WG1 Leads for the Road to Dallas

May 12–13, 2027 Target

The AI Networking Summit Dallas (May 12–13, 2027, Hyatt Regency Frisco) carries the theme “Proving the AI Infrastructure Fabric.” To build WG1’s 2027 delivery schedule, we need WG1 leadership input on five questions:

Decision Area Context & Options Question for WG1 Leads
1. Sequencing the 7 Section 6 Specifications for Dallas Section 6 names seven deliverables, with the Rights & Normal-Behavior Spec / Rights-BOM (Task 1, UC #6/#13/#15) and the 5-SLI Promotion Scorecard & Portable Regulator Proof (Tasks 2 & 4, UC #4/#14/#19) having the highest board vote concentration (38 of WG1’s 42 votes). Which 2–3 specifications should WG1 target for v1.0 publication and live demonstration at Dallas in May 2027, and which belong in H2 2027?
2. Onboarding Board Submitters as Spec Co-Authors Seven board members submitted use cases absorbed into WG1: Denis Mandich (#15), James Walker (#3, #4), Parantap Lahiri (#19), Satish Natarajan (#9), Vesko Pehlivanov (#13, #14), Travis Ewert (#20), and Baird Kaake (#6). How should we structure small drafting sub-teams within WG1 so each board submitter co-authors their corresponding specification section?
3. Next onug-plane Reference Implementation Proof (P4/P5) onug-plane already proves credential isolation (P1), WORM journaling (P2), and drift quarantine/stop-all (P3). New board asks add the 5-SLI Nutrition Label computation (UC #19), control-bypass harness (UC #14), and per-call jurisdiction policy (UC #20). Which proof scenarios should we build next in onug-plane for the Dallas main stage and vendor challenge?
4. WG1 ↔ WG5 Post-Quantum Handshake WG5 will define post-quantum SPIFFE SVIDs and ML-DSA signed A2A Cards/AIBOMs, and onboard a Crypto-Discovery & Migration persona onto WG1’s plane. Who from WG1 should serve as the technical liaison to WG5 so WG5’s post-quantum identity profile drops cleanly into WG1’s Agent Trust Fabric and Registry/BOM schema?
5. WG1 ↔ WG3 Forensic Journal & Attribution Interface WG3 (AI Agents as Insider Threats) will rely on WG1’s gateway journal for chain-of-custody forensics and human-vs.-agent attribution. Are any additional span fields (such as delegating human principal, session intent hash, or network encryption posture from UC #20) needed in WG1’s journal schema to support WG3’s insider investigations?
