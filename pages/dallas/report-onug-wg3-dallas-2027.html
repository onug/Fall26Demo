1. WG3 Charter

Updated Working Group

WG3 treats AI agents as insiders. Agents hold real access, work inside trusted boundaries, and act on their own. WG1 decides what an agent may do and blocks violations at the gateway. WG3 takes the cases that enforcement doesn’t catch:

WG3 OwnsWhat It Means
In-policy misuseA sanctioned agent misuses its legitimate access through compromise, manipulation, or its own behavior, and every action still passes policy.
Shadow agentsEmployees launch agents on laptops, browsers, or VMs under their own credentials. The agent never passes through the gateway, and logs record the employee.
Insider-risk programsDetection, investigation, forensics, and response for agents, and extending enterprise insider-risk programs (Security, HR, Legal, Privacy) to cover them.

The Evidence: Anthropic Form S-1 (Filed October 26, 2026)

“Four of our models gained unauthorized access to real third-party systems during sessions lasting roughly 10 to 34 hours of active work.” (p. 48) No attacker was involved. The agents were pursuing their assigned goals.

The 2026 SOC persona work is not lost. It moves to WG2, which shares the same v0.6 architecture.

2. What the Board Prioritized in NYC (October 27)

8 Votes · 1 Use Case

None of the 20 Board use cases asked for an AI-assisted SOC, so WG3 refocused on the one Board ask no other group owns. The UC #5 submitter will be invited to co-author or serve as a Board Advisor to WG3.

Use CaseVotesRole in WG3
UC #5 Unsanctioned Agents Acting for Employees8Anchor. Employee-launched agents acting under human credentials are insiders by definition.
UC #20, scenario 5 Proving Control in Sovereign & Regulated Environments—Supporting. Agent incident attribution. The use case and its votes sit with WG1 and WG5.
UC #2, agent side Cross-Domain Telemetry & Incident Attribution—Supporting. When WG2 finds an incident was agent-induced, WG3 investigates the agent. The use case and its votes sit with WG2.

That gives WG3 three candidate tracks:

A. Shadow Agents
Find unsanctioned agents on endpoints, browsers, and VMs. Backed by UC #5 (8 votes).
B. Attribution
Reconstruct which agent did what, on which path, under which policy. Supports UC #20 and UC #2.
C. Program Guide
Extend enterprise insider-risk programs to agents. No Board vote; fills a gap in CISA guidance.

WG3’s base is thin: one use case and four vendor members carried over from other groups. Recruiting members is part of its first-year work.

4. Potential Paths Forward

Decision for WG3 Leads

All three tracks are 2027 work. The choice is what WG3 publishes and demonstrates in Dallas, and what moves to the second half of 2027.

ONUG leadership is considering another working demo for Dallas. A demo can go with any of the three paths, and each card shows what it would demonstrate. No WG3-specific capability runs in the reference implementation today. WG1’s signed journal, which WG3 investigations would draw on, does.

Path 1 · Board-backed (UC #5)

Shadow Agents First

Dallas: Track A. Publish a playbook for finding unsanctioned agents on laptops, browsers, and VMs.

For: The only track with Board votes behind it. Concrete, and every enterprise has the problem.

Against: Needs endpoint and browser visibility vendors WG3 doesn’t have yet.

Demo: A browser agent running under an employee’s login is found and identified as an agent, not the person. Not built.

Path 2 · Supports UC #20 & #2

Attribution First

Dallas: Track B. Publish an agent attribution and forensics standard.

For: Builds on WG1’s signed journal, which runs today, and ties WG3 tightly to WG1 and WG2.

Against: Depends on journal fields WG1 hasn’t agreed yet, and on use cases owned by other groups.

Demo: A sanctioned agent is manipulated into sending out records within its allowed limit. An investigator rebuilds the timeline from the journal. The journal runs today; the investigation workflow is not built.

Path 3 · No Board vote

Program Guide First

Dallas: Track C. Publish a guide for extending enterprise insider-risk programs to agents.

For: No build needed. CISA’s 2026 guide still defines an insider as a person, so the gap is real, and the guide speaks to CISOs and insider-risk leaders WG3 needs to recruit.

Against: No Board votes behind it, and nothing to demo live.

Demo: A tabletop exercise in place of a live demo: Security, HR, and Legal walk through an agent insider incident.

Questions for WG3 Leads

1. PathWhich path does WG3 take to Dallas?
2. DemoIf ONUG runs a WG3 demo in Dallas, is it a live demo or a tabletop exercise, and what can realistically be built before May?
3. Board AdvisorsThe UC #5 submitter will be invited to co-author or serve as a Board Advisor to WG3. Should we also invite the UC #2 and UC #20 submitters to advise WG3 on attribution? Who on the leads team makes each invitation, and how often do Board Advisors meet with the group?
4. MembersWhich enterprise insider-risk leaders (CISOs, insider-threat program owners, forensics leads) and vendors (identity, endpoint and browser visibility, DLP, insider risk) should we invite to join WG3?
5. LiaisonsWho from WG3 works with WG1 on the journal fields investigators need, and with WG2 on handing over the SOC persona?
