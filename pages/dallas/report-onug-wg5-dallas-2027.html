1. WG5 Charter

New Working Group

WG5 makes AI infrastructure quantum-safe. Agent security runs on signatures: certificates, tokens, and Agent Cards prove who an agent is and whom it acts for. Signatures are the hardest part of the post-quantum migration, and post-quantum signatures are up to 50 times larger. Agent protocols are being written now, so they can be built quantum-safe from the start. For most enterprises, vendor roadmaps set the timeline, because most of the cryptography sits inside products they don’t control.

2027 DeliverableWhat It Covers
1. Procurement requirementsWhat enterprises should require of vendors: dated PQC roadmaps, cryptographic bills of materials, configurable algorithms, and end-of-support notice. First in the charter.
2. Agent identity profilePost-quantum versions of agent identities, Agent Cards, and signed models. WG1 keeps the identity model; WG5 defines the cryptography it uses.
3. Migration playbookAn AI agent that finds quantum-vulnerable cryptography and proposes fixes for human review, running on WG1’s control plane.
4. Key-management guidanceKeeping keys where they belong, rotating them, and failing closed when a key service goes down, across edge, on-prem, and cloud.

WG5’s scope is post-quantum cryptography, not quantum key distribution.

3. The Road to Dallas

May 12–13, 2027 · Hyatt Regency Frisco

The AI Networking Summit Dallas 2027 runs May 12–13 at the Hyatt Regency Frisco. Post-Quantum Cryptography has its own track, and its description reads like WG5’s charter.

Proving the AI Infrastructure Fabric

“Build the Foundation. Enable the Fabric. Power the AI Future.”

The PQC track says “PQC has stopped being a future mandate and become a present-day procurement requirement,” and that “the actual bottleneck isn’t the cryptography, it’s crypto-asset discovery.” Those are WG5’s first and third deliverables. Dallas lands eight weeks after CISA and NIST publish the cryptographic bill of materials minimum elements.

4. Potential Paths Forward

Decision for Founding Leads

All four deliverables are 2027 work. The choice is what WG5 brings to Dallas, five months after forming, and what moves to the second half of 2027.

ONUG leadership is considering another working demo for Dallas. Each card says what WG5 could show. Agents in the onug-plane reference implementation use standard, not post-quantum, identities today, so any live post-quantum demo needs building.

Path 1 · Serves all 5 use cases

Procurement First

Dallas: Publish enterprise PQC procurement requirements.

For: Matches the Dallas PQC track’s framing almost word for word, and serves every WG5 use case. No build needed, and it can cite the March 19 bill of materials elements.

Against: Not where the Board’s votes concentrated, and nothing to demo live.

Demo: None needed. Could pair with Path 2’s tabletop.

Path 2 · 9 of 16 votes

Key Resiliency First

Dallas: Publish key-management guidance for distributed AI infrastructure.

For: Follows the Board’s votes. UC #16 is the Board’s fifth-ranked use case overall and WG5’s largest.

Against: Needs HSM and key-management vendors WG5 has yet to recruit.

Demo: A tabletop exercise with enterprise security leaders: a regional key service goes down, and the room works through whether agents fail closed and how they recover. A live version on onug-plane would need building.

Path 3 · 2 of 16 votes

Post-Quantum Agents Live

Dallas: Publish the agent identity profile, with a live proof on onug-plane.

For: The most visible option and the tightest link to WG1. It measures what post-quantum signatures, up to 50 times larger, cost on every agent handshake.

Against: The fewest Board votes, and the most build work.

Demo: Either agents running on post-quantum identities with handshake benchmarks, or a discovery agent that inventories vulnerable cryptography and proposes fixes for review. Neither is built.

Questions for Prospective WG5 Leads

1. PathWhich path does WG5 take to Dallas? Paths 1 and 2 can combine, since neither needs a live build.
2. DemoIf ONUG runs a WG5 demo in Dallas, is it a tabletop exercise, a live onug-plane proof, or both? If live, which proof first: post-quantum agent identities or the discovery agent?
3. Board AdvisorsEach Board submitter will be invited to co-author their track or serve on it as a Board Advisor to WG5. The UC #10 and UC #20 submitters could advise both WG5 and the group holding the other half. Who on the leads team makes each invitation, and how often do Board Advisors meet with the group?
4. Founding MembersWhich enterprise security and cryptography leaders, and which PQC, PKI, key-management, and network vendors, do we invite as founding members?
5. LiaisonsWho from WG5 works with WG1 on the agent identity profile, with WG2 on testing rollouts in the twin, and with WG4 on encryption overhead on AI WAN links?
