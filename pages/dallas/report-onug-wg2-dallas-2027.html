1. WG2 Charter

Continuing Working Group

WG2 defines how AI agents run enterprise operations safely. Every agent follows the same six-step loop: Detect, Diagnose, Propose, a gate check, Execute, and Observe & Learn. No change goes ahead until it passes the gate. WG2 does not rebuild identity, credentials, or audit logging; its agents run on WG1’s control plane. The v0.6 reference architecture was ratified October 15, 2026.

PersonaThe Gate Question
NOC (network operations)Verify Gate: Will this change break the network?
SOC (security operations)Decide Gate: Contain the threat now, or keep watching? New to WG2 in 2027, moved from WG3.

Two operational domains sharing one loop is the Board paper’s test in practice: the next domain onboards without new enforcement code.

4. Potential Paths Forward

Decision for WG2 Leads

All three tracks are 2027 work. The choice is what WG2 publishes and demonstrates in Dallas, and what moves to the second half of 2027.

ONUG leadership is considering another working demo for Dallas. A demo can go with any of the first three paths, and each card shows what it would demonstrate. Path 4 is a demo-led option proposed by a WG2 member. “Running today” means already running in the reference implementation: a three-switch lab network with a network source of truth and two NOC agents, one that observes and recommends and one that makes bounded changes.

Path 1 · 17 of 37 votes

Safe Change First

Dallas: Track B only. Publish the design-intent agent and pre-change Verify Gate specs.

For: The most Board votes of any track, and it builds on the agents and lab already running.

Against: Leaves the Board’s #2 use case (UC #2) without a Dallas deliverable.

Demo: An agent explains why the network was built as it was, proposes a change, and the change is tested in a digital twin and checked against the live change window before it runs. The agents and lab run today; intent analysis and twin validation need to be built.

Path 2 · 15 of 37 votes

Attribution First

Dallas: Track A only. Publish a vendor-neutral profile for correlating telemetry across carrier, enterprise, and cloud networks.

For: Leads with the Board’s #2 use case. Brownouts with no clear owner are a familiar NOC problem.

Against: Needs telemetry the lab doesn’t have today: carrier optical, cloud gateways, and DNS. That depends on vendor members supplying it.

Demo: A brownout where every domain reports green, attributed to its root cause in minutes. Not built.

Path 3 · 32 of 37 votes

Attribution + Safe Change

Dallas: Tracks A and B, joined into one end-to-end story.

For: Covers 86% of WG2’s votes and shows the whole loop, from finding the fault to fixing it safely. Track C follows naturally in the second half.

Against: The most build work of the three, split across two sub-teams.

Demo: Path 2’s brownout is diagnosed, a fix is proposed, tested in the twin, and executed. Only the agents and lab run today.

Path 4 · 26 of 37 votes · Proposed by a WG2 member

Demo-Led: Slow Bank Teller Transaction

Dallas: A 10-minute live demo of one incident, from customer complaint to fix. A teller transaction that normally takes about 2 seconds takes 9 to 12. Application, database, network, SD-WAN, and cloud agents work together to find the cause: the database is healthy, but a network path change added latency between application services. The network operations agent proposes moving teller traffic to the preferred SD-WAN path. WG1’s control plane runs the Verify Gate (agent identity, least privilege, policy, digital-twin validation, impact analysis), then issues 60-second credentials for the change. Transaction time drops back to about 2 seconds, and the outcome is logged. The demo ends with one or two failure scenarios, such as a wrong diagnosis or an agent reaching outside its scope, to show the controls stopping it. Specs follow in the second half of 2027.

For: A customer-facing story any executive understands, from a member’s real environment. It shows UC #2 (finding the cause across domains), UC #12 and #18 (testing the change before it runs), and UC #10 (the full loop) in one sequence. It is also a joint demo with WG1: application and network agents collaborate, but the control plane stays the source of authority. That fits the summit’s “Proving” theme and the AI Automation track.

Against: The most build work of the four paths. onug-plane needs an application stack (teller UI, API, account service, database), two SD-WAN paths, and application, database, and cloud agents. Today it has three switches and two NOC agents. The scenario’s eight steps also need to map to the v0.6 six-step loop. UC #17 (design intent) is not in the scenario yet; adding a step where an agent explains why the preferred path is preferred would bring it in.

Demo: This path is the demo, run on the onug-plane reference implementation. Its WG1 controls (agent identity, short-lived credentials, the gateway, the signed journal, and fleet-wide stop) and its network lab and NOC agents run today. The application stack, SD-WAN paths, and the new agents are extensions to build. The proposal suggests a four-panel live dashboard: customer experience, cross-domain evidence, the control plane, and the incident timeline.

Questions for WG2 Leads

1. PathWhich path does WG2 take to Dallas, and which vendor members anchor each track?
2. DemoIf ONUG runs a WG2 demo in Dallas, do we commit to it? Is the teller scenario (Path 4) the demo, and which vendor members supply the application stack, SD-WAN, and telemetry it needs before May?
3. Board AdvisorsEach Board submitter will be invited to co-author their track or serve on it as a Board Advisor to WG2. Who on the leads team makes each invitation, and how often do Board Advisors meet with the group?
4. SOC PersonaWho leads the SOC persona inside WG2, and how do we settle the open question of how much the NOC and SOC agents can read of each other’s data?
5. LiaisonsWho from WG2 works with WG4 on the line between operations and WAN transport, and with WG5 on testing post-quantum rollouts in the twin?
