The v0.6 drawing behind the WG2 requirements document, redrawn in ONUG's design language at full detail. WG3's SOC architecture uses the identical skeleton — see the WG3 reference diagram for where the two diverge.

How to read this drawing

Plan, then build. Nothing in the enforcement box can be built until the three persona-creation decisions are made — the autonomy level decides whether the loop's action steps ever fire, and the model-location decision carries its own retry and contamination risk.

Two access patterns, one enforcement standard. Direct and orchestrated are both valid; the orchestrator is drawn deliberately without extra authority — it checks policy on every request like any other path. Agents/MCPs is a governed resource exactly like the other three, because "we've automated the silos" only gets fixed if cross-domain agent reachability is controlled, not open.

Color repeats the read/write split from the requirements document. Steps 1–3 are green and read-only; steps 4–6 are orange and only fire if the persona's autonomy level allows it. Execute is the one box in the entire drawing that can mutate anything.

Where this feeds the requirements document

The three persona bands are PER-1, PER-2, PER-3. The two access patterns and four resource tiles are ACC-1, ACC-2 and RES-1–4. The six loop steps are LOOP-1–6. The invariants at the bottom are the Boundary constraints table. Every label on this page has a numbered requirement in the WG2 requirements document, each traced further back to one of WG1's 25 items in the crosswalk.
