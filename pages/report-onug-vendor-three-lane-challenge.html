The Vendor Three-Lane Challenge

WG1 — Agentic Control Plane· WG2 — Autonomous Infrastructure· WG3 — AI-Enabled SOC· 2026-09-17· owner: ONUG Collaborative
Three working groups, three lanes, one standard: prove it, don't claim it.

Every vendor briefing to WG1, WG2, or WG3 gets measured against the same question, asked three different ways depending on which lane the product sits in. This is that question, made explicit. A vendor that can answer all three "prove it" columns with a working demo — not a roadmap slide — is speaking the working groups' language.

Why nine controls, not a slogan

Each of the nine numbered controls already has a home in that lane's working-group requirements document — the mapping above isn't new scope, it's a translation of what's already required into a control a vendor can be asked to demonstrate by number.

  • Lane 1 carries five controls, Lanes 2 and 3 carry two each — the split isn't even because the plane (Lane 1) is where most of the accountability machinery actually lives; NOC and SOC (Lanes 2–3) each add only what's specific to running inside that domain.
  • Lane 1 is the plane itself — not a fourth thing WG1 does alongside the plane, but the accountability restated as numbered controls: #1–5 are already numbered requirements in the WG1 requirements document.
  • Lanes 2 and 3 are what the plane looks like from inside a domain — the same invariants (mediated writes, gated decisions, an audit trail the agent can't touch), now phrased as controls a NOC or SOC operator would actually ask a vendor to demonstrate.

For vendors preparing a briefing

Bring the lane that matches your product to a live demo, not a slide. "Prove it" means the working group sees the kill switch fire, the rollback execute, or the chain of custody survive — in the room, on your product, not on a roadmap.
